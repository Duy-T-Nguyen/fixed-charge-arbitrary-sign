\title{Fixed charges of arbitrary sign: what survives and what fails}
\author{Duy T. Nguyen\corref{cor1}}
\ead{duynguyen.das@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author. ORCID: \href{https://orcid.org/0009-0004-0732-2093}{0009-0004-0732-2093}}
\address{Independent Researcher, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam}

\begin{abstract}
Fixed-charge models assume the charge is paid, not received. Wholesale electricity
clears at negative prices, and a negative price turns an activation charge into a
payment received. We ask what survives. The vertex argument does: for a fixed-charge
integer program whose continuous constraint matrix is totally unimodular, an optimal
solution is an integral vertex of the cell obtained by conditioning on its support,
whatever the signs. That argument is elementary; what is not is where it becomes necessary.
A fixed charge is piecewise concave in Zangwill's sense exactly when it is paid, so the
classical machinery stops precisely at the sign change. The structural
properties it is usually invoked to deliver do \emph{not} survive. The vertex bound
constrains only coordinates lying strictly between $1$ and their upper bound, and we
exhibit instances with negative charges whose unique optimum runs every activity it
uses at the minimum level $1$: the fractional-period property of Florian--Klein and Love,
the spanning-forest bound for fixed-charge transportation and the spanning-tree bound for
fixed-charge flow each fail. Algorithms resting on the classical properties therefore
cannot simply be carried over. The $O(T^4)$ recursion of Akbalik, Penz and Rapine is
correct under the concavity it assumes; applied outside it, we give a four-period
instance on which its state space cannot represent the optimum and it loses $16.7\%$. Total unimodularity cannot be dropped. The same analysis exposes
a modelling error in the natural mixed-integer formulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Write the cost of using activity $j$ at level $x\in[0,u_j]$ as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fc}
\varphi_j(0)=0,\qquad \varphi_j(x)=s_j+c_jx\quad(x>0),
\end{equation}
with a fixed charge $s_j$ and a marginal rate $c_j$. In applications both are a price
multiplied by a technical coefficient, $s_j=p_j\sigma_j$ and $c_j=p_j\gamma_j$ with
$\sigma_j,\gamma_j>0$, so both inherit the sign of $p_j$. Wholesale electricity clears
below zero, and increasingly so as variable renewable capacity grows \cite{seel21}.
There a fixed charge is received rather than paid.

The sign of $s_j$ decides the curvature. For $x>0$ and $0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord}
\varphi_j(\lambda x)-\lambda\varphi_j(x)=s_j(1-\lambda),
\end{equation}
so $\varphi_j$ is concave on $[0,u_j]$ exactly when $s_j\ge0$. When $s_j<0$ it lies
strictly below its own chord, which makes it \emph{convex}; what \eqref{eq:fc} loses at
the origin is not convexity but \emph{lower semicontinuity}, since
$\liminf_{x\downarrow0}\varphi_j(x)=s_j<0=\varphi_j(0)$.

That distinction matters, because concavity and lower semicontinuity are exactly the
hypotheses the structure theory runs on. Florian and Klein \cite{fk71} assume concavity;
Love \cite{love73} and Swoveland \cite{swoveland75} assume piecewise concavity. Koca,
Yaman and Akt\"urk \cite{kya14} assume piecewise concavity together with lower
semicontinuity. Akbalik, Penz and Rapine \cite{apr15} invoke Love's property explicitly
under concave costs. The same model has a polyhedral literature \cite{ak05}, which does
not use the structural property at all; and the concave case with arbitrary capacities is
NP-hard \cite{flr80}, so the polynomial results are all for restricted capacities. Ou \cite{ou17} imposes no sign restriction in his model statement,
but the structural property he uses is attributed to Swoveland and is proved there for
piecewise concave costs, so the hypothesis is inherited by the proof.

Two qualifications are due at once. First, \cite{kya14} already anticipate a repair:
their \S3.4 models minimum order quantities by setting $p_t(x)=\infty$ on
$(0,L)\cup(C,\infty)$, and solves that case in $O(n^6)$. With $L=1$ the effective domain
becomes $\{0\}\cup[1,C]$, which restores lower semicontinuity and leaves a piecewise
concave cost whose breakpoints $\{0,1,C\}$ are time-invariant and fixed in number ---
precisely the condition their polynomial algorithm requires. Negative charges are
therefore not outside their framework; they are outside the framework as it is usually
instantiated. The polynomial solvability of the $L=1$ case in \cite{kya14} is not at issue
here. What fails, and what Sections~\ref{sec:fails}--\ref{sec:alg} are about, is the
transfer of the classical structural properties, and of the algorithms built on them, to
charges of either sign.
Second, \cite{love73} is closer than it first appears. His model already carries
production bounds $x^L_i\le x_i\le x^U_i$, and his conclusion excepts production equal to
``zero or its upper or lower bound''; with $x^L_i=1$ that exception set is $\{0,1,x^U_i\}$,
which is exactly the conclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:count} below. What the corollary adds
is that this holds \emph{without} concavity --- Love assumes his cost concave on
$[0,\infty)$, which \eqref{eq:fc} is not when $s_j<0$. The classical property in the usual
$x^L_i=0$ setting is a different statement, and Section~\ref{sec:fails} shows it fails.

In itself, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is three lines --- conditioning on the support
leaves an \emph{affine} function on a bounded polyhedron whose vertices are integral by
Hoffman--Kruskal --- and we claim no depth for it. But the standard route to a result of
this shape is genuinely closed, and Proposition~\ref{prop:zangwill} shows why.
Zangwill \cite{zangwill67} is the canonical reference here, and it is how \cite{love73}
reaches the lot-sizing result. His theory is not confined to continuous costs: his
Theorem~6 minimises any \emph{piecewise concave} function over its dominant set, and such
functions may jump. Proposition~\ref{prop:zangwill} shows that \eqref{eq:fc} lies in that
class precisely when $s_j\ge0$, so the sign of the charge is the exact boundary of the
theory rather than an obstruction we happen to meet.

\paragraph{Contribution.} What is new here is negative and, we think, more useful.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:count} survive arbitrary signs.
Section~\ref{sec:fails} shows that the three classical structural properties they are
invoked to deliver do not, with a witness in each setting whose \emph{unique} optimum
violates the classical statement. Section~\ref{sec:alg} carries the failure into an
algorithm: the $O(T^4)$ recursion of \cite{apr15}, correct under the concavity it
assumes, cannot be transferred beyond it, and loses $16.7\%$ on a four-period instance. Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp} shows total unimodularity cannot be dropped.
Remark~\ref{rem:model} records a modelling error the same fact makes visible.

\section{The problem class}

Let $A$ be a totally unimodular matrix, $b$ an integral right-hand side,
$u\in\Z_{>0}^n$, and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
(\mathrm{FC})\qquad
\min\ \Phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_j(x_j)
\quad\text{over}\quad x\in\Z^n,\quad Ax\lesseqgtr b,\quad 0\le x\le u,
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_j$ as in \eqref{eq:fc} and $s_j,c_j\in\R$ \emph{of arbitrary sign}.
Write $\supp(x)=\{j:x_j>0\}$, and for $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ define the \emph{cell}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell}
P(S)=\bigl\{x\in\R^n:\ Ax\lesseqgtr b,\quad x_j=0\ (j\notin S),\quad 1\le x_j\le u_j\ (j\in S)\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The constant $1$ in \eqref{eq:cell} is where integrality enters, and it is the source of
everything in Section~\ref{sec:fails}: for continuous variables the set
$\{x_j>0,\ j\in S\}$ is not closed and carries no vertex guarantee.

\section{The vertex theorem}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cell}
Let $x^{*}$ be feasible for \eqref{eq:P} and $S=\supp(x^{*})$. Then $x^{*}\in P(S)$;
every integral point of $P(S)$ is feasible for \eqref{eq:P} with support exactly $S$;
$\Phi$ agrees on $P(S)$ with the affine function $\sum_{j\in S}s_j+\sum_{j\in S}c_jx_j$;
and $P(S)$ is a non-empty bounded polyhedron all of whose vertices are integral.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$x^{*}$ is integral with $x^{*}_j\ge1$ on $S$, so $x^{*}\in P(S)$. Conversely any
$x\in P(S)$, integral or not, has $x_j\ge1>0$ exactly on $S$, hence
$\Phi(x)=\sum_{j\in S}(s_j+c_jx_j)$; the first sum is constant on $P(S)$, so this is
affine there. Boundedness is immediate from $0\le x\le u$. The constraint matrix of
$P(S)$ is $A$ with rows of the identity adjoined, which preserves total unimodularity,
and every right-hand side --- entries of $b$, $u$, $0$ and $1$ --- is integral; by
Hoffman--Kruskal the polyhedron is integral.
\end{proof}

The affineness is the whole of the argument. The only part of $\Phi$ that is neither
convex nor concave \emph{as a whole} is the switch at the origin, and conditioning on
the support switches it off: once we know which activities are used, the fixed charges
are a constant and nothing non-linear remains. No curvature is needed, so no sign
restriction is needed either.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Problem \eqref{eq:P} has an optimal solution $\hat x$ that is an integral vertex of
$P(\supp(\hat x))$, for fixed charges and marginal rates of arbitrary sign.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $x^{*}$ be optimal and $S=\supp(x^{*})$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cell}, $P(S)$ is a
non-empty bounded polyhedron on which $\Phi$ is affine, so $\Phi$ attains its minimum
over $P(S)$ at a vertex $\hat x$, and $\Phi(\hat x)\le\Phi(x^{*})$. Again by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell}, $\hat x$ is integral and feasible with support exactly $S$, so
$\Phi(\hat x)\ge\OPT=\Phi(x^{*})$. Hence $\hat x$ is optimal and is a vertex of
$P(S)=P(\supp(\hat x))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:count}
Let $\hat x$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let $A_{\mathrm{t}}$ be the rows of $A$
tight at $\hat x$, and let $Q=\{j:1<\hat x_j<u_j\}$. Then the columns of
$A_{\mathrm{t}}$ indexed by $Q$ are linearly independent; in particular
$|Q|\le\operatorname{rank}A_{\mathrm{t}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $d\ne0$ is supported on $Q$ with $A_{\mathrm{t}}d=0$. For small $\varepsilon>0$
the points $\hat x\pm\varepsilon d$ satisfy the tight rows with equality and the slack
ones by continuity; the bounds hold because every coordinate of $Q$ is strictly
interior; and coordinates outside $S$ are untouched, since $Q\subseteq S$. So $\hat x$
is the midpoint of a segment in $P(S)$, contradicting that it is a vertex.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zangwill}
Let $X=[0,u_j]$ and let $\varphi_j$ be as in \eqref{eq:fc}. Then $\varphi_j$ is piecewise
concave in the sense of \cite{zangwill67} \emph{if and only if} $s_j\ge0$. His Theorem~6
therefore applies to a fixed charge that is paid and to no fixed charge that is received.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Zangwill calls $F$ \emph{continuous piecewise concave} (CPC) on $X$ when
$F=\max_i g_i$ for finitely many continuous concave $g_i$, with \emph{basic sets}
$B_i=\{z\in X: F(z)=g_i(z)\}$ and \emph{terminal set} $T=\bigcup_{a\ne b}(B_a\cap B_b)$; and
$F$ is \emph{piecewise concave} (PC) when it is the pointwise limit of CPC functions
$\{F_k\}$ that share one terminal set $T$ and satisfy $F_k=F_1$ on $E[X]\cup T$.

Suppose $\{F_k\}$ is such a sequence for $\varphi_j$. Each $F_k$ is a maximum of finitely
many continuous functions, hence continuous; and $F_k=F_1$ on $E[X]\cup T$ together with
$F_k\to\varphi_j$ pointwise gives $F_1=\varphi_j$ there.

Since $0\in E[X]$ we get $F_1(0)=\varphi_j(0)=0$. As $F_1$ is continuous and
$s_j+c_jx\to s_j\ne0$ as $x\downarrow0$, choose $\delta\in(0,u_j)$ with $|F_1|<|s_j|/2$ and
$|s_j+c_jx|>|s_j|/2$ on $[0,\delta]$. Any $x\in T\cap(0,\delta)$ would satisfy
$F_1(x)=\varphi_j(x)=s_j+c_jx$, violating both bounds; hence $T\cap(0,\delta)=\emptyset$.

Fix $k$. The basic sets of $F_k$ are closed and cover $X$, and no two of them meet in
$(0,\delta)$, which is connected; so exactly one basic set meets $(0,\delta)$ and $F_k$
agrees there with a single continuous concave $g^k$. By continuity,
$g^k(0)=F_k(0)=F_1(0)=0$. Concavity with $g^k(0)=0$ gives, for $x\in(0,\delta)$ and
$\lambda\in(0,1)$,
\[
g^k(\lambda x)\ \ge\ \lambda g^k(x)+(1-\lambda)g^k(0)\ =\ \lambda g^k(x).
\]
Letting $k\to\infty$ and using $g^k\to\varphi_j$ pointwise on $(0,\delta)$,
\[
s_j+c_j\lambda x\ \ge\ \lambda(s_j+c_jx),\qquad\text{that is}\qquad s_j(1-\lambda)\ \ge\ 0 ,
\]
so $s_j\ge0$, contradicting $s_j<0$.

Conversely let $s_j\ge0$, put $K_0=s_j/u_j+|c_j|+1$ and
\[
F_k(x)\ =\ \min\bigl\{(k+K_0)x,\ s_j+c_jx\bigr\},\qquad k\ge1 .
\]
Each $F_k$ is a minimum of two affine functions, hence continuous and concave, hence CPC as
a family with the single member $F_k$ itself. That family has no second member, so no two
basic sets meet and the terminal set is $\emptyset$ for every $k$; condition~(2) then only
asks for agreement on $E[X]=\{0,u_j\}$. There $F_k(0)=\min\{0,s_j\}=0=\varphi_j(0)$, using
$s_j\ge0$, and $(k+K_0)u_j>s_j+c_ju_j$ for every $k\ge1$ by the choice of $K_0$, so
$F_k(u_j)=s_j+c_ju_j$ independently of $k$. Finally, for fixed $x\in(0,u_j]$ we have
$(k+K_0)x>s_j+c_jx$ once $k$ is large, so $F_k(x)\to s_j+c_jx$, while $F_k(0)=0$; thus
$F_k\to\varphi_j$ pointwise and $\{F_k\}$ is a defining sequence.
\end{proof}

The sign of the fixed charge is therefore not merely a technical obstacle but the exact boundary
of the class Zangwill's theory covers. That is why the classical route is available for the
received wisdom and closed here, and it is the precise sense in which a charge that is
received is a different object rather than the same object with a hypothesis relaxed.

\section{What does not survive}\label{sec:fails}

Corollary~\ref{cor:count} constrains only the coordinates with $1<\hat x_j<u_j$. The
classical structural properties constrain those with $0<\hat x_j<u_j$. Under
non-negative charges the difference is immaterial in practice, because a positive fixed
charge penalises opening an activity and optima consolidate. Under negative charges it
is the whole story: a negative fixed charge \emph{rewards} opening, so optima open
everything they can and drive volumes to the minimum. Each witness below has a
\emph{unique} optimum, so no choice of optimal solution repairs the classical statement,
and each respects the coupling $s_j=p_j\sigma_j$, $c_j=p_j\gamma_j$ with
$\sigma_j,\gamma_j>0$ of Section~1 --- the failures are not an artefact of letting the
fixed charge and the marginal rate carry opposite signs.

\paragraph{Lot sizing.} Let $x_t$ be production in period $t$, $u_t=C$, and let the rows
of $A$ be the prefix sums bounded below by cumulative demand and above by cumulative
demand plus a storage bound; $A$ is an interval matrix, hence totally unimodular. Take
$T=3$, $C=3$, zero demand, storage bound $2$, and prices $p=(-1,-1,1)$ with
$\sigma_t=\gamma_t=1$, so $s_t=c_t=p_t$. The unique optimum is $x=(1,1,0)$, with prefix
sums $(1,2,2)$ and value $-4$. Rows $2$ and $3$ are tight and row $1$ is not, so no
inventory point separates periods $1$ and $2$, both of which produce strictly between $0$
and $C$: the fractional-period property of \cite{fk71,love73} fails on the unique
optimum. Here $Q=\emptyset$, so Corollary~\ref{cor:count} holds vacuously.

\paragraph{Fixed-charge transportation.} Let $A$ be the $2\times2$ bipartite incidence
matrix with supplies and demands $(2,2)$, $u_{ij}=2$ and $s_{ij}=c_{ij}=-1$. The unique
optimum, of value $-8$, sends one unit on each of the four arcs. No arc is strictly interior, so
again Corollary~\ref{cor:count} is vacuous, while the four positive arcs exceed the
classical bound $m+n-1=3$ and form a cycle.

\paragraph{Fixed-charge min-cost flow.} The same instance read as a flow problem on four
nodes with $b=(2,2,-2,-2)$ gives four positive arcs against the spanning-tree bound
$n-1=3$.

So the sign hypothesis cannot be removed from the classical statements; it can only be
removed from the vertex statement, which is weaker. Section~\ref{sec:alg} shows the
difference is not academic.

\section{An algorithm that breaks}\label{sec:alg}

Corollary~\ref{cor:count} is sometimes read as a licence: an algorithm whose correctness
uses non-negativity only through a vertex argument should keep working when the charges
go negative. The licence is void whenever the algorithm uses the \emph{classical}
property rather than the vertex property, and that is the common case.

The recursion of \cite{apr15} is $O(T^4)$, is correct under the concave costs it assumes,
and builds its state space directly on Love's property --- which it states, after
\cite{love73}, in terms of periods that are \emph{fractional} in the classical sense
$0<x_t<P$: within a subplan it assumes exactly $K=D_{u,v-1}/P$ periods produce at full
capacity $P$ and one fractional period absorbs the remainder. Take $T=4$, $C=5$, storage
bound $4$, demand $(0,0,0,4)$ and $s_t=c_t=-1$. Exhaustive enumeration gives optimal
value $-12$, attained by a plan using level $1$ in several periods. The best plan the
subplan decomposition can represent has value $-10$: an error of $16.7\%$. The state
space cannot express the optimum, so no amount of care in the recursion recovers it.

\begin{remark}
A second instance in the same family, $T=5$, $C=4$, reports a relative error of
$564.5\%$. We do not quote it as the headline: its optimal value is $-0.31$, so the
ratio is inflated by a near-zero denominator, and its \emph{absolute} loss of $1.75$ is
smaller than the $2.00$ above. Relative errors on objectives that change sign are not
informative.
\end{remark}

\section{Total unimodularity cannot be dropped}\label{sec:sharp}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sharp}
There is an instance of \eqref{eq:P} without total unimodularity whose unique optimum is
not a vertex of its cell, so the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} fails.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take the odd-cycle system $x_1+x_2\le6$, $x_2+x_3\le7$, $x_1+x_3\le6$, whose constraint
matrix has determinant $2$, with $u=(5,5,5)$, $s=(1.32,\,2.51,\,0.55)$ and
$c=(-2.02,\,-2.06,\,-1.19)$. Exhaustive enumeration over the $113$ feasible integer
points gives the unique optimum $\hat x=(2,4,3)$ of value $-11.47$, the runner-up being
$(3,3,3)$ at $-11.43$. Its support is $\{1,2,3\}$, so its cell is
$\{x:\ Ax\le b,\ 1\le x\le5\}$; the first two rows are tight there and no bound is, so
the tight system has rank $2<3$ and $\hat x$ is not a vertex.
\end{proof}

This shows some hypothesis is needed, not that total unimodularity is the weakest one;
whether balanced or perfect matrices suffice is open.

\begin{remark}
The weaker odd-cycle instance one first reaches for --- $x_i+x_j\le3$ with $1\le x\le3$
and objective $-\sum_jx_j$ --- does \emph{not} serve. Its cell has continuous optimum
$-4.5$ at $(\tfrac32,\tfrac32,\tfrac32)$ while the best integral point is $-4$ at
$(1,1,2)$, so the affine-minimum step reaches a fractional vertex; but $(1,1,2)$ is
itself a vertex of the cell, and the conclusion survives. That example breaks the
argument, not the theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A modelling consequence]\label{rem:model}
The natural mixed-integer formulation of \eqref{eq:fc} writes $x_j\le u_jy_j$ with $y_j$
binary. For $s_j\ge0$ that suffices, because setting $y_j=1$ with $x_j=0$ only adds
cost. For $s_j<0$ it does not: the solver sets $y_j=1$ and $x_j=0$ and banks the charge.
Over integer $x$ the constraint $y_j\le x_j$ is required. On a four-period instance with
prices $(1,-5,-5,-5)$, two units to be served by the horizon, $u=2$ and unit
coefficients, the guarded formulation returns $-20$ and the unguarded one $-25$: the
unguarded model reports a cost $25\%$ below the truth, and its solution ``uses'' idle
activities purely to collect the charge. The object it optimises is the lower
semicontinuous hull of $\varphi_j$, which is the function \cite{kya14} assume and
\eqref{eq:fc} is not.

The unguarded pattern is the standard one. \cite{apr15} write $0\le x_t\le Py_t$ with no
lower link, and \cite{lcw01} write $x_t\le My_t$ having just \emph{defined}
``$y_t=1$ if $x_t>0$, and $0$ otherwise'' --- a two-sided definition implemented by a
one-sided constraint. Both are correct in their own settings, where the setup cost is
non-negative and $y_t=1$ with $x_t=0$ is never optimal; we report no error in either.

Two qualifications keep this in proportion. First, the repair is not new where the issue is
already live: in energy models the minimum-load constraint plays exactly this role, as in
$p_t\ge P^{\min}z^{\mathrm{on}}_t+P^{\mathrm{sb}}z^{\mathrm{sb}}_t$ of
\cite{raheli23}, which forbids an activated unit from consuming nothing. What is missing
from the lot-sizing formulations above is a constraint the energy literature writes as a
matter of course. Second, the general shape --- an indicator pinned on one side only, correct
because of the sign of its coefficient --- is widespread and not confined to
\eqref{eq:fc}. The same paper \cite{raheli23}, whose numerical study is explicitly about
nonpositive prices, defines its start-up binary by
$z^{\mathrm{su}}_t\ge z^{\mathrm{off}}_{t-1}+z^{\mathrm{on}}_t+z^{\mathrm{sb}}_t-1$ with no
upper pin, which is correct only because the start-up cost is a positive constant rather
than a price. Our point is about \eqref{eq:fc}; the pattern is broader.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational verification}

Every number below is produced by a named script released with this note, runnable with
no arguments. Two of them corrected claims in earlier drafts of this work.

\paragraph{The theorem.} Over $399$ lot-sizing instances with $T\in\{4,\dots,6\}$,
$C\in\{4,\dots,8\}$ and charges drawn from $[-3,3]$, enumerating \emph{every} optimal
plan: no violation of Corollary~\ref{cor:count}, the bound biting on $219$ of them
(\texttt{redo\_sec7.py}). Over $1{,}085$ instances whose matrices were \emph{checked} to
be totally unimodular, with mixed $\le/\ge/=$ rows and $u\in\{1,2,3\}$: no violation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (\texttt{check\_thm1\_tu.py}).

\paragraph{How often the classical property fails.} Over $3{,}000$ random lot-sizing
instances with prices of arbitrary sign and the coupling of Section~1 imposed
($s_t=p_t\sigma$, $c_t=p_t\gamma$, $\sigma,\gamma>0$), the fractional-period property
fails on \emph{every} optimum in $215$ of them, or $7.2\%$; releasing the coupling raises
this only to $8.6\%$, so the coupling is not what protects the property
(\texttt{sign\_coupling.py}). Separately, Corollary~\ref{cor:count} is vacuous
($Q=\emptyset$ at every optimum) on $11.5\%$ of mixed-sign instances against $9.2\%$ of
non-negative ones (\texttt{check\_vacuity.py}). The failures are a minority, but a
substantial one, and an algorithm must be correct on all instances.

\paragraph{Beyond the stated cost class.} The argument extends to per-period costs that
are piecewise linear by conditioning on which piece each coordinate falls in as well as
on the support. One point deserves care. If the coefficients of adjacent pieces are
unrelated the cost \emph{jumps} at each breakpoint, so the pieces are half-open exactly
as $(0,u_j]$ is, and integrality closes them: the piece containing a used coordinate is
$[\beta_k+1,\beta_{k+1}]$, and $[1,\beta_1]$ for the first. Those are rows of the identity
with integral right-hand side, so total unimodularity survives. Taking the pieces as
$[\beta_k,\beta_{k+1}]$ instead does \emph{not} work, and produced violations until the
bounds were corrected. With two interior breakpoints, $T\in\{4,5\}$, $C\in\{6,7,8\}$ and
all four coefficient families drawn independently: $0/200$ violations with non-negative
coefficients, $0/200$ with arbitrary signs, $0/200$ with arbitrary signs and no storage
bound, the check biting on $158$, $172$ and $191$ of them respectively
(\texttt{check\_piecewise.py}). Independently, $266$ piece-conditioned cells were solved
as linear programs and none has a fractional optimum (\texttt{L1\_L2.py}).

\paragraph{The steps of the proof.} That $\Phi$ is affine on $P(S)$ was verified on
$1{,}269$ same-support pairs, worst error $3.6\times10^{-15}$; as a control, without which
the argument would be vacuous, allowing the support to vary breaks affinity on $217$ of
$220$ instances (\texttt{check\_affine.py}). The condition of Corollary~\ref{cor:count}
was compared against the rank of the full active constraint system on all $6{,}088$
feasible plans of $120$ lot-sizing instances, agreeing in every case
(\texttt{check\_vertex\_rank.py}).

\section{Concluding remarks}

The vertex statement survives arbitrary signs by an elementary argument for which we claim
no depth --- though Proposition~\ref{prop:zangwill} shows the classical route to it is
closed, and that the sign of the charge is exactly where Zangwill's class ends. What we think matters is that the
statements it is usually invoked to deliver do not survive, that this is visible in all
three classical settings, and that a published polynomial recursion --- correct under
its own hypotheses --- cannot be transferred beyond them, losing optimality on an
instance of four periods. The practical reading is the reverse of the one a vertex
argument invites: a negative fixed charge does not merely relax a hypothesis, it changes
which plans are optimal, from few-and-large to many-and-minimal.

Three directions remain. Whether the class extends beyond totally unimodular continuous
parts is open. Proposition~\ref{prop:zangwill} characterises the summand $\varphi_j$,
which is the object the classical hypotheses are stated about, and its necessity half lifts
to $\Phi$ with $s_j$ replaced by the charge that \emph{switches on}. Let $z$ be a vertex of
$X$ and $d$ a feasible direction there, and put
\[
J(z,d)\ =\ \textstyle\sum\{\,s_j:\ z_j=0,\ d_j>0\,\}.
\]
Then $\Phi(z+td)\to\Phi(z)+J(z,d)$ as $t\downarrow0$, and running the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:zangwill} along the ray --- $z$ is pinned because $z\in E[X]$, the
terminal set avoids a neighbourhood of it, and one concave piece is active on the rest ---
gives $J(z,d)\bigl(1-\lambda\bigr)\ge0$. So $\Phi$ fails to be piecewise concave as soon as
some $J(z,d)$ is negative, the one-dimensional statement being the case $z=0$, $d=e_j$,
$J=s_j$. Both witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:fails} fail this way, the transportation one at
the vertex $(0,2,2,0)$ along $(2,-2,-2,2)$, where $x_{11}$ and $x_{22}$ switch on together
and $J=-2$. Whether $J(z,d)\ge0$ everywhere is also \emph{sufficient} we have not
determined. And of the formulations we checked, the two lot-sizing ones \cite{apr15,lcw01} omit the
guard of Remark~\ref{rem:model} --- harmless where they stand, not where they would be
reused --- while the energy model \cite{raheli23} carries its continuous analogue as a
minimum-load constraint. A search for a published formulation that both prices a fixed
charge at a signed quantity and omits the guard returned nothing, so the exposure we
describe is at present a property of how these models would be combined rather than one we
can exhibit in print.
